What is the Google Drive daily upload limit (750 GB)?

Published 29 October 2026 · Updated 29 October 2026 · By Sai Nath

Google says each Google Workspace user can upload and copy 750 GB to Drive in 24 hours, across My Drive and all shared drives. After that, uploads and copies stop until 24 hours have passed. A 3 TB job therefore needs at least four days, and five if uploaded in safe batches of 700 GB.

This guide comes from the maker of Uploft, a Chrome extension. Every Google limit below links to Google's own pages and was checked on 29 October 2026. Google can change them, so the linked page wins if it differs.

How is the 750 GB counted?

Per user, not per file and not per folder. Google's Drive API limits page says users can upload 750 GB per day between My Drive and all shared drives, and that the limit also applies to copies. Its Workspace limits page puts it as 750 GB uploaded and copied within 24 hours, and says the limit refreshes within 24 hours. Google's pages describe a window of 24 hours, not a reset at midnight, so a plan should not rely on a fixed clock time.

Two other limits sit beside it and are easy to confuse with it. A single file can be up to 5 TB, and a file can only be copied up to 750 GB. See all the Drive upload limits in one table.

What happens to a file in progress?

The file that crosses the limit is the last one to finish. Google's pages say only the first file that breaks the limit completes uploading, and that a user who reaches 750 GB, or uploads a file larger than 750 GB, can't upload or copy more files until 24 hours have passed. Files queued behind it are refused.

An illustration, inferred from that wording rather than tested: with 700 GB already sent today, a 100 GB file can still start and finish, taking the day to 800 GB. The next file is then refused. A single 900 GB file is allowed to complete once, and nothing more goes up for 24 hours afterwards.

How to spread an upload over days

Divide the total by 750 GB and round up. For 3 TB (3,000 GB): 3,000 ÷ 750 = 4 days. Because the file that crosses the limit still finishes, batches of a little under 750 GB leave a margin; in batches of 700 GB the same job is 3,000 ÷ 700 = 4.3, so five days.

  1. Sort the files into batches whose sizes add up to under 750 GB each.
  2. Upload one batch, then wait until Google lets uploads through again, which its pages say is within 24 hours.
  3. Check the storage: the limit is daily, but the total still has to fit in the account's storage.

Time matters too. At 100 Mbps, 750 GB takes 750 × 8,000 megabits ÷ 100 Mbps = 60,000 seconds, about 16 hours 40 minutes, so on a home connection the speed can be the tighter limit. See how to upload 1 TB to Google Drive.

Does it apply to Workspace and personal accounts?

Google documents it for Google Workspace users. Its help page for personal accounts, Files you can store in Google Drive, lists the 5 TB file size and no daily figure, so this guide states the 750 GB limit as Google's Workspace limit and does not promise the same number for a personal account. The Workspace upload limit guide covers it.

What does Uploft do about it?

It does not raise or bypass the limit. Uploft is a free Chrome extension (Chrome 116 or later) with public source code. When Drive refuses an upload for the daily limit, the uploader stops that file and shows "Drive daily upload limit reached (750 GB). Try again tomorrow" instead of retrying a failure that cannot succeed. The uploader keeps its queue across a closed tab or restart, so the remaining files can be continued once Drive accepts uploads again.

Uploft is free, and its source code is public. It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet; the home page shows what it does and what it asks for.
