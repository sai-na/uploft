Why is my Google Drive upload stuck, and what do I do?

Published 29 October 2026 · Updated 29 October 2026 · By Sai Nath

A Google Drive upload is usually stuck because the connection dropped, the Drive tab was closed or put to sleep, or your Google storage is full. Check storage first, then keep the tab open and the computer awake and click Retry. Where it stops, at 0%, in the middle or at 100%, points to the cause.

This guide comes from the maker of Uploft, the Chrome extension mentioned below. Google's limits and steps link to Google's own pages and were checked on 29 October 2026; what the extension does comes from its source code.

Stuck at 0%, mid-way or at 100%: which is it?

Start with where the upload stopped. Google does not publish a list of what each Drive upload label means, so the table below gives the likely causes from how uploads work, and each row leads to a page for that symptom. Account limits come first in every case: if storage is full, no retry helps.

What you seeLikely causeTry first
Stuck at 0% Storage full, no connection, or the upload never started Check Storage, then the network
Stops part-way The connection dropped, the computer slept or the tab was unloaded Wait for the network, then click Retry
Stuck at 100% The file is sent and Drive is still finishing it Give it a few minutes, keep the tab open
Waiting or paused Queued behind other files, or the upload was paused See the section below
Preparing to upload Drive is reading the file or the connection is slow to start See the section below
Stops again and again An unstable connection, sleep or a daily limit See the section below

For the full checklist of failures (storage, the 750 GB daily limit, the tab, the file itself), see Google Drive upload failed or stuck? Causes and fixes. This page is about telling the symptoms apart.

Two account limits explain a stop that no retry fixes. Google says that when an account reaches its storage limit, "you won't be able to upload or create files in Drive". Google also documents a 750 GB limit on what one Google Workspace user can upload and copy to Drive in 24 hours. In Drive, click Storage in the left-hand menu to see what you use.

Waiting or paused

A file that waits is usually queued behind others, and a paused one was stopped on purpose or by a lost connection. Look at the other files in the panel first: if some are moving, the waiting ones are in line. If none are moving, work through the connection and the tab.

  1. Check the Wi-Fi or cable. Open any other page to confirm the internet works.
  2. Keep the drive.google.com tab open. Closing it, quitting Chrome or restarting the computer ends an upload started there.
  3. Stop Chrome unloading the tab. Open Settings ▸ Performance, click Add next to "Always keep these sites active" and add drive.google.com. Google says Memory Saver "deactivates tabs that you aren't currently using", and its list of tabs it leaves active (audio or video, active downloads, partially filled forms, pinned tabs and a few more) does not mention uploads.
  4. Click Retry on a failed file, or reload the page and upload the file again if nothing moves.

Preparing to upload

An upload stuck on "Preparing" has not started sending yet. Google does not explain this label, so treat it as a start that has not got going: the connection is slow or down, the file is on a drive that is slow or disconnected, or the page needs reloading. Check that the file opens on your computer, check the network, then cancel the file and add it again.

If many files all sit on "Preparing", the account is the more likely cause. Check Storage before anything else.

Keeps stopping

An upload that fails over and over is almost always losing the tab, the connection or the computer's power state, because the page sending it has to stay alive for the whole transfer. The longer the file, the more chances of a stop: at 20 Mbps, a 50 GB file takes about 5 hours 33 minutes (50 × 8,000 megabits ÷ 20 Mbps = 20,000 seconds).

  • Plug the computer in and change its sleep setting so it stays awake while uploading.
  • Prefer a cable or a strong Wi-Fi signal for big files.
  • Split a very large upload into batches so one drop costs less.
  • If it fails at the same point every time, check storage and the 750 GB daily limit, which no retry gets past.

For an upload that does not depend on an open tab, Google makes Google Drive for desktop for Windows and macOS. Google says you can pause or resume its sync at any time.

What the extension fixes and what it can't

Uploft is a free Chrome extension (Chrome 116 or later, on a computer) that targets the causes above.

  • Auto-retry. On drive.google.com it clicks Drive's own Retry button after 5, 20 and 60 seconds, and waits for the network if the computer is offline.
  • Keep-awake and no tab discarding. It asks Chrome to keep the computer awake from inactivity while uploads run, and marks the uploading tab so Chrome does not discard it automatically.
  • A progress panel on the Drive tab and a notification when uploads finish or fail.
  • A stalled-piece restart in its own uploader: a piece that moves no bytes for 60 seconds is restarted, and the upload continues from what Drive already has.

It cannot fix a full Google storage or the daily limit, and it cannot change what Drive does after Retry. Keep-awake does not stop a closed laptop lid or Sleep from sleeping the computer. It is not on the Chrome Web Store yet.

Uploft is free, and its source code is public. It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet; the home page shows what it does and what it asks for.
