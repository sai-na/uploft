Are Google Drive upload limits different on a work or school account?

Published 29 October 2026 · Updated 29 October 2026 · By Sai Nath

Yes, in the details. A work or school account follows Google Workspace's limits: files up to 5 TB and 750 GB uploaded or copied per user in 24 hours. Storage is pooled and can be capped per user by an administrator, and an administrator can also block apps and extensions from reaching Drive.

This guide comes from the maker of Uploft, a Chrome extension. Every Google limit below links to Google's own pages and was checked on 29 October 2026. Google can change them, so the linked page wins if it differs.

How do Workspace and personal limits differ?

The per-file and per-day figures are the same where Google documents them; storage and who controls it are what change. Google's Workspace page says users can "upload and synchronize files up to 5 TB" and that each user can upload and copy 750 GB to Drive within 24 hours. Its page for personal accounts lists the 5 TB file limit and no daily figure, so a personal account's daily cap is not stated there.

Can an admin set a lower storage quota?

Yes. Google's storage settings page says an administrator sets a baseline per-user limit on the top organisational unit, then overrides it for child units or groups, and that group limits always override organisational-unit limits. A user who is over their limit can't upload new files to Drive. Shared drives have their own limit, 100 GB by default, and a shared drive over its limit can't take new files or edits. Without any admin limit, use is bounded only by the organisation's pooled storage.

So "storage full" on a school account can mean the student's own cap, not Google's. The settings are in the Admin console under Storage, then Manage; Google's page is Set storage limits. Only an administrator can see or change them, so the fix is to ask the administrator, or to free up space. The daily upload limit guide covers the other cause of a stopped upload.

Can an admin block apps and extensions?

Yes, for apps that sign in with Google. In the Admin console, under Security, Access and data control, API controls, an administrator marks an app Trusted, Limited or Blocked, and can set Google Drive as a restricted service. Google's page says a blocked app "can't access any Google service", and that for Drive the high-risk scopes include full Drive access. The administrator can optionally let untrusted apps use scopes that aren't high-risk. Google's page for this is Control which third-party and internal apps access Google Workspace data.

Uploft asks for one Google permission, drive.file. Google's developer documentation lists that scope as non-sensitive, and describes it as access to files the app creates or that the user opens or shares with it through a picker. Whether a given organisation lets an app with that scope sign in depends on its own settings, which this page can't see.

Can a school or work account use the extension?

Not yet for most people. Uploft is not on the Chrome Web Store, and its Google sign-in is in Google's "Testing" mode: only Google accounts on the owner's list of test users can sign in until the owner publishes the app. That applies to every account type, work, school or personal. Once it is published, a work or school account can use it only if the administrator allows it, as the section above explains. The extension also needs Chrome 116 or later on a computer.

It does not change any limit. On a work or school account it hits the same 5 TB, 750 GB and storage caps as Drive's website, and it stops and names the cause, for example "Your Google storage is full" or "Drive daily upload limit reached (750 GB). Try again tomorrow", rather than retrying an upload that can't succeed. Where Drive for desktop is permitted, it is Google's own way to sync files with Drive from a computer; see Google's Drive for desktop help.

Uploft is free, and its source code is public. It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet; the home page shows what it does and what it asks for.
