How do I upload files and folders to Google Drive from a computer?

Published 29 October 2026 · Updated 29 October 2026 · By Sai Nath

Go to drive.google.com, click New at the top left, choose File Upload or Folder Upload, then pick what to send. You can also drag files or folders into an open Drive folder. Both methods upload through the browser tab, so keep it open. Google Drive for desktop uploads from a folder on your computer instead.

This guide comes from the maker of Uploft, the Chrome extension mentioned near the end. Google's steps link to Google's own help page and were checked on 29 October 2026; what the extension does comes from its source code.

How do I upload files with the New button?

  1. On your computer, go to drive.google.com and sign in.
  2. Open the folder you want the files in, or stay in My Drive.
  3. At the top left, click New, then File Upload.
  4. Select one or more files in the window that opens and confirm.
  5. Wait until Drive shows each file as uploaded.

Can I drag and drop files instead?

Yes. Google's steps: open or create the destination folder in Drive, then drag the files or folders into it. It is the same browser upload as the New button, so the same rule applies: closing the tab or quitting Chrome stops it.

How do I upload a whole folder?

Click New, then Folder Upload, and select the folder. Drive uploads the folder with its files. Dragging a folder into an open Drive folder does the same.

Should I use Drive for desktop instead?

It depends on whether you want to watch the upload or leave it. Google Drive for desktop is Google's own app for Windows and macOS. Per Google's upload page, once it is installed you drag files or folders into the "Google Drive" folder on your computer and they sync to Drive. Because the app does the uploading, no browser tab has to stay open. It is the right choice when you want a folder on the computer to stay in step with Drive. For a one-off batch, the browser is quicker to start, and needs nothing installed.

Where do uploaded files go?

Into the folder you are in when you upload. Google's page says you can upload to private or shared folders, and that uploads use your Drive storage even when the folder belongs to someone else. If a file with the same name already exists in that folder, Drive adds the new one as a new version of the original; to keep both, click Keep both files after uploading.

When is an extension worth it?

When the upload is big or long, and you do not want to babysit the tab. Uploft is a free Chrome extension (Chrome 116 or later, source code public) with two parts. On drive.google.com it shows a progress panel, keeps the computer awake, stops Chrome unloading the tab and clicks Drive's Retry button for you. Its own uploader page opens from the toolbar icon, signs in with Google and has Choose files and Choose folder buttons, plus a drop area. It sends 1 to 6 files at a time (4 by default) and, if a connection drops or Chrome restarts, continues from what Drive already has. For files that already exist in Drive, you pick Skip it, Replace it or Keep both.

More: why uploads fail, resuming an upload and large files.

What it does not do

  • It does not make one file upload faster than Drive's own site: your connection's upload speed is the limit.
  • Skip and Replace only see files the extension uploaded itself.
  • It runs in Chrome on a computer, not on phones, and it is not on the Chrome Web Store yet. Google sign-in is in testing, so only listed test users can sign in for now.
  • You do not need it for a few small files: the New button is enough.

Uploft is free, and its source code is public. It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet; the home page shows what it does and what it asks for.
