Can Google Drive keep uploading in the background on a computer?

Published 29 October 2026 · Updated 29 October 2026 · By Sai Nath

Yes, but only while the upload's own page stays open. Drive's website sends a file from its tab, so switching to another tab or window is fine, while closing the tab or quitting Chrome ends the upload. Google's own app that works without a browser is Google Drive for desktop.

This guide comes from the maker of Uploft, the Chrome extension mentioned below. Google's limits and steps link to Google's own pages and were checked on 29 October 2026; what the extension does comes from its source code.

What keeps running when you switch tabs?

The upload does, as long as the tab is still open and Chrome keeps it loaded. A tab that is out of sight is still a running tab. The risk is Chrome's Memory Saver. Chrome's help says it "deactivates tabs that you aren't currently using" and that opening such a tab again reloads it. Google lists some activity that stops a tab being deactivated: audio or video, screen sharing, downloads and pinned tabs. Uploads are not on that list. You can add a site under Settings ▸ Performance ▸ "Always keep these sites active".

Uploft marks the tab it is uploading in as not discardable while files are sending, so Chrome does not unload it.

What happens if I close the browser?

On Drive's website, the upload ends, because the page that was sending it is gone, and you upload the file again. Quitting Chrome, a crash and a restart all have the same effect. Drive itself can carry on a resumable upload from the last byte it received for up to a week, but that needs an uploader that saved its place; see how to resume a Google Drive upload. In Uploft's uploader, uploads also stop when the tab closes, and the page asks before you leave while they run. What survives is the saved list: reopen the uploader and the interrupted files can be continued.

Does the computer have to stay awake?

Yes. A computer that sleeps stops sending, whatever the program. While uploads run, the extension asks Chrome to stop the computer sleeping from inactivity, using Chrome's chrome.power API, and releases the request when the uploads finish. It is switchable in the toolbar popup under "Keep the computer awake". It only stops sleep caused by inactivity, so a sleep you ask for, such as choosing Sleep from a menu, still happens. After a sleep, the interrupted piece is sent again and the file continues. See why uploads stop when the computer sleeps.

What does Drive for desktop do?

Google Drive for desktop is Google's own app. It runs on the computer, not in a browser tab. Google says that edits to files "sync automatically to your computer and to Google Drive", and that you "can pause or resume the sync process ... at any time". It suits a folder you want kept in sync all the time. It is not a way to send one large batch and then close things: it syncs chosen folders, and Google does not say how it resumes a half-sent file.

How do I upload without watching the browser?

Keep the uploader tab open and let it look after the rest. With Uploft:

  1. Click the toolbar icon and choose Upload files to Drive.
  2. Use Choose files or Choose folder, or drop them on the page.
  3. Set At a time (1 to 6 files; 4 by default) and what to do if a file is already in Drive.
  4. Leave the tab open and do something else, in another tab or window.

It resends after a network drop, waits while the computer is offline, and carries on after a closed tab from the saved queue. On drive.google.com, the extension also presses Drive's Retry button for failed uploads and keeps the computer awake, but an upload started there still ends if its tab is closed.

What are the limits?

  • A browser upload needs its tab open; the extension cannot upload after the tab is closed.
  • The computer must be awake and online. It carries on after a drop, but not while asleep.
  • One file's pieces go in order, so a single file uploads at about the speed of Drive's own site.
  • Runs in Chrome 116 or later on a computer. It is not on the Chrome Web Store yet.

Uploft is free, and its source code is public. It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet; the home page shows what it does and what it asks for.
