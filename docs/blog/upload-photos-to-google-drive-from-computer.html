How do I upload lots of photos to Google Drive from a computer?

Published 29 October 2026 · Updated 29 October 2026 · By Sai Nath

Go to drive.google.com, click New ▸ Folder upload and choose the folder that holds your photos; Drive uploads everything inside it. For a large library, Uploft, a Chrome extension, sends 1 to 6 photos at a time, skips ones already in Drive on a second run and carries on after a dropped connection.

This guide comes from the maker of Uploft, the Chrome extension mentioned below. Google's steps link to Google's own pages and were checked on 29 October 2026; what the extension does comes from its source code.

How to upload photos to Google Drive from a computer

  1. On your computer, go to drive.google.com.
  2. At the top left, click New, then Folder upload.
  3. Choose the folder with your photos. To send single photos, choose File upload instead.
  4. Keep the tab open until Drive's upload panel says it has finished.

These are the steps on Google's help page, Upload files & folders to Google Drive, which lists images among the files you can upload. Uploaded photos count against your Drive storage. Google says that the storage of a Google Account is shared by products such as Google Photos, Google Drive and Gmail. This guide is about Drive, not about Google Photos backup.

With Uploft, open the uploader from the toolbar popup (Upload files to Drive), click Choose folder or drop the folder in, and leave If already in Drive on Skip it. The photos go up as they are: the extension does not resize or convert them.

Can I upload a photo folder in order?

Mostly. The extension adds the photos to its queue in the order it reads the folder and starts them in that order, and each photo keeps its path, such as Photos/2024/IMG_0412.jpg, so the same year folders are created in Drive. With At a time set to 1, photos finish one after another in queue order. With 3 or 6, several are in flight at once and a small photo can finish before a larger one that started earlier, so the order they finish in is not guaranteed.

The uploader sends each photo's modified time from your computer along with it. Folders you choose keep their folder names, and hidden files such as .DS_Store, Thumbs.db and desktop.ini are left out. How folder upload works.

How do I avoid duplicates on a second run?

Choose the same folder again with Skip it selected. Before sending a photo, the uploader looks in the destination folder for a file with the same name and the same size in bytes. If there is one, it is marked Already in Drive, skipped and nothing is sent. A photo with the same name but a different size is uploaded as a second file.

A worked case: you uploaded 1,200 photos last month and have added 80 since. Choosing the folder again queues 1,280; 1,200 are skipped and 80 are uploaded. The other settings are Replace it (a new version of the same Drive file) and Keep both. Google's page says Drive's own website, given a same-name file, uploads it as a revision of the file already there. What counts as already in Drive.

Why are thousands of photos slow?

Because the total size sets the time, and a library is large. Uploads go at your connection's upload speed, not its download speed. 4,000 photos of 5 MB each is 4,000 × 5 = 20,000 MB, or 20 GB. On a 20 Mbps upload, 20 × 8,000 = 160,000 megabits, and 160,000 ÷ 20 = 8,000 seconds, which is 2 hours 13 minutes.

Photos are many mid-sized files, so sending several at once helps: the At a time setting runs 1 to 6 files side by side, 4 by default. It cannot raise your upload speed, so if one photo already fills the connection, six at once share the same total. Uploading many files at once.

Can it upload in the background?

Partly. Drive's website uploads from the tab, so closing the tab ends the upload. Google's own background route is Drive for desktop, which uploads without a browser tab; see Use Google Drive for desktop.

The extension's uploader also runs in a Chrome tab, but it can be left alone: with Keep the computer awake while uploading on (the default), it asks the computer not to sleep from inactivity, it retries a failed photo after a wait that grows from 1 second to 60, and it carries on from the last byte Drive confirmed. After a closed tab or a restart, press Resume; Chrome may ask permission to read the folder again. How resuming works.

Limits to know

  • The uploader runs in a Chrome tab on a computer. Closing the tab pauses the queue until you press Resume. Closing a laptop lid can still put the computer to sleep.
  • Skip it only knows files the extension uploaded itself, because it uses Google's narrowest Drive permission (drive.file). Photos you added through drive.google.com are not seen, so a copy is uploaded beside them.
  • Skip it matches name and size, not content. It does not find a renamed copy.
  • One photo is limited by your upload speed. The gain comes from sending several photos at once, not from a faster connection.
  • It does not back up to Google Photos or watch a folder for new photos.
  • It runs in Chrome 116 or later, it isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet, and the Google sign-in is in testing, so only listed test users can sign in for now.

Uploft is free, and its source code is public. It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet; the home page shows what it does and what it asks for.
