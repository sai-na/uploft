Why is my Google Drive upload failing or stuck?

Published 29 October 2026 · Updated 29 October 2026 · By Sai Nath

Google Drive uploads fail or stall for four common reasons: the connection dropped, the computer slept or Chrome unloaded the tab, your Google storage is full, or a Workspace account hit its 750 GB daily limit. Check your storage, keep the Drive tab open and the computer awake, then click Retry. Closing the tab ends the upload.

This guide comes from the maker of Uploft, the Chrome extension mentioned below. Google's limits and steps link to Google's own pages and were checked on 29 October 2026; what the extension does comes from its source code.

Check these first

Work down the list. The first two are account limits that no retry gets past; the rest are about the connection, the tab and the file.

  1. Your storage. In Drive, click Storage in the left-hand menu to see what you use. Google says that once you reach your limit, "you won't be able to upload or create files in Drive". A personal Google Account includes up to 15 GB, shared by Drive, Gmail and Photos.
  2. The daily limit. A Google Workspace user can upload and copy 750 GB to Drive within 24 hours. After that, uploads fail until the limit refreshes, which Google says happens within 24 hours.
  3. The connection. If the Wi-Fi dropped, wait until it's back, then click Retry on the failed file in Drive's upload panel.
  4. The tab. Keep the drive.google.com tab open until the upload finishes. Closing it, quitting Chrome or restarting the computer ends the upload.
  5. Sleep and Memory Saver. Keep the computer awake. In Chrome, open Settings ▸ Performance and, next to "Always keep these sites active", click Add and add drive.google.com. Chrome's Memory Saver "deactivates tabs that you aren't currently using", and Google's list of tabs it leaves alone (audio, downloads, half-filled forms and a few more) doesn't mention uploads.
  6. The file itself. If one file fails every time while others go up, check that it opens on your computer and isn't on an external drive or network folder that disconnected.

Why does it stop when I close the tab or the computer sleeps?

Because an upload you start on drive.google.com is sent by that web page. Close the tab, quit Chrome or restart the computer, and the page is gone, so the upload stops with it. A sleeping computer stops its network connection, which interrupts the upload the same way.

For uploads that don't depend on an open tab, Google makes Google Drive for desktop for Windows and macOS. You put files in its Google Drive folder and it syncs them; Google says you can pause or resume the sync at any time.

Why do large files fail more often?

Because they are exposed for longer. Drive accepts files up to 5 TB, but the tab, the computer and the connection all have to hold for the whole upload. At an upload speed of 20 Mbps, a 50 GB file takes about 5 hours 33 minutes: 50 × 8,000 megabits ÷ 20 Mbps = 20,000 seconds. A short drop is far more likely to land somewhere in five hours than in five minutes. The guide to large uploads has the times for other sizes and speeds.

What does the 750 GB daily limit mean?

It is a cap on how much one Google Workspace user can upload and copy in 24 hours: 750 GB across My Drive and shared drives. Google says users who reach it can't upload or copy more files until the limit refreshes, and that only the first file that breaks the limit completes uploading. Google's help page for personal accounts lists a 5 TB maximum file size and no daily figure.

How does Uploft help?

Uploft is a free Chrome extension (Chrome 116 or later) that works on both kinds of upload: the ones you start on drive.google.com, and its own uploader.

Uploads you start on drive.google.com

These need no sign-in. While Drive's upload panel is busy, the extension:

  • Retries for you. When an upload fails, it clicks Drive's own Retry button after 5, 20 and 60 seconds. If the computer is offline, it waits for the network and tries 3 seconds after it returns. A failure notification comes only after the last try.
  • Keeps the computer awake. It asks Chrome to stop the computer sleeping from inactivity while uploads run, and releases that when they finish.
  • Stops the tab being unloaded. It marks the uploading Drive tab so Chrome doesn't discard it automatically to save memory.
  • Shows progress. A progress panel with speed and time left, and a notification when uploads finish or fail.

Retry and keep-awake are on by default. Turn either off from the extension's toolbar icon: Keep the computer awake and Retry failed Drive uploads. What Drive does after Retry is up to Drive; the extension only presses the button.

Its own uploader, which resumes

Click the extension's toolbar icon, then Upload files to Drive, and sign in with Google. The uploader sends files with Drive's resumable uploads:

  • After a dropped connection it asks Drive how much of the file arrived and continues from that byte.
  • While the computer is offline, files show "Waiting for the network", and that time doesn't count as failed tries.
  • A piece that moves no bytes for 60 seconds is restarted.
  • Server errors and rate limits are retried after about 1, 2, 4 seconds and so on, up to a minute apart. After 20 failed tries in a row with no progress, the file is marked failed.
  • Problems a retry can't fix stop at once with a plain reason, such as "Your Google storage is full" or "Drive daily upload limit reached (750 GB). Try again tomorrow".

Closing the uploader tab or restarting Chrome stops its uploads too, but they don't start over: reopen the uploader and they continue from what Drive already has. See how resuming a Drive upload works.

What can't it fix?

  • A full Google storage or the daily limit. Those are Google's limits.
  • An upload in Drive's own tab still ends if you close that tab. Only the extension's uploader picks up after a closed tab.
  • Keep-awake prevents sleep from inactivity, as Chrome's power API describes it. Closing a laptop's lid or choosing Sleep still puts the computer to sleep.
  • Speed. One file goes up at about the speed of Drive's own site, because your connection's upload speed is the limit.
  • It runs in Chrome on a computer, not on phones, and it isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet.

Uploft is free, and its source code is public. It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet; the home page shows what it does and what it asks for.
