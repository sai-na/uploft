What are the Google Drive upload limits?

Published 29 October 2026 · Updated 29 October 2026 · By Sai Nath

A Google Drive file can be up to 5 TB. Each user can upload or copy 750 GB in 24 hours, a folder outside a shared drive holds up to 500,000 items, and a shared drive holds up to 500,000 items in all. Storage is 15 GB on a personal account and depends on the plan for Workspace.

This guide comes from the maker of Uploft, a Chrome extension. Every Google limit below links to Google's own pages and was checked on 29 October 2026. Google can change them, so the linked page wins if it differs.

The rows come from Google's Workspace limits page, its shared drive limits page and its Drive API limits page. The sections below cover each limit and say where Google's pages differ by account type.

How big can one file be?

Up to 5 TB. Google's help page on files you can store lists "Up to 5 TB" for files other than Docs, Sheets and Slides, and its Workspace page says users can upload and sync files up to 5 TB. A file larger than 5 TB can't be uploaded. Copying has a lower ceiling: a file larger than 750 GB can't be copied, so it has to be downloaded and uploaded again. The file size limit guide goes into it.

A bigger file is exposed to a dropped connection for longer. At 20 Mbps, a 50 GB file takes about 5 hours 33 minutes: 50 × 8,000 megabits ÷ 20 Mbps = 20,000 seconds. See how to upload large files.

How much can you upload per day?

750 GB per user in 24 hours, copies included. Google's Drive API page says a user who reaches it, or uploads a file larger than 750 GB, can't upload or copy more files for 24 hours, and the Workspace page says only the file that breaks the limit finishes uploading. The 750 GB is counted across My Drive and all shared drives. Google's personal-account help page lists the 5 TB file limit and no daily figure, so check your account type before planning around it. More in the daily upload limit guide.

How many items fit in a folder or shared drive?

A folder outside a shared drive holds up to 500,000 items directly in it; the root of My Drive is exempt, and subfolder contents don't count towards the parent. A shared drive holds up to 500,000 items in all, counting files, folders, shortcuts and items in Trash. Folders nest up to 100 levels deep, and in the 100th folder you can add files and shortcuts but not more folders. On shared drives, Google shows a warning banner when less than 20% of the item limit remains.

How much storage do you get?

A personal Google Account includes up to 15 GB, shared among Drive, Gmail and Photos. Items in Trash and Spam still count until cleared, and Google says storage can take 48 to 72 hours to update after a large delete. Once you reach the limit, in Google's words, "you won't be able to upload or create files in Drive". Workspace storage is pooled and depends on the edition, for example Google lists 30 GB times the number of users for Business Starter and 2 TB times the number of users for Business Standard.

What are the Docs, Sheets and Slides conversion limits?

When Drive converts an uploaded file to a Google format, smaller limits apply. Google's help page lists these:

  • Google Docs: up to 1.02 million characters, and a converted text document up to 50 MB.
  • Google Sheets: up to 20 million cells or 100 MB for a spreadsheet created in or converted to Sheets. In an Excel conversion, cells with more than 50,000 characters are removed.
  • Google Slides: a presentation converted to Slides can be up to 100 MB.

A file kept in its original format, such as a .xlsx or .pptx, falls under the 5 TB file limit instead.

What does Uploft change?

None of these limits. Uploft is a free Chrome extension (Chrome 116 or later) with public source code. It can't raise a Google limit or get around one. What it does is keep a long upload alive up to the limit: its uploader sends files with Drive's resumable uploads, carries on after a dropped connection, a closed tab or a restart, and sends one to six files at a time (four by default).

When a limit is the reason, it stops at once and says so, with messages such as "Your Google storage is full" or "Drive daily upload limit reached (750 GB). Try again tomorrow", instead of retrying a failure that can't succeed.

Uploft is free, and its source code is public. It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet; the home page shows what it does and what it asks for.
