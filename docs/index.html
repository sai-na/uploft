Made for uploads that take a while

Hours of camera footage, a photo library, a backup folder. The kind of upload you start, then walk away from.

When Wi‑Fi drops, it waits instead of failing

Files go up in pieces that Drive confirms one at a time. When the connection goes, the uploads in progress switch to Waiting for the network and continue from the last confirmed piece as soon as it's back.

Other hiccups, like Drive asking it to slow down, are retried with growing pauses for about 15 minutes before a file is marked as failed.

Closed the tab? Reopen it and pick up where it stopped

The queue is saved in your browser. After a closed tab, a crash or a restart, the uploader shows what was interrupted and how much Drive already has, then sends only the rest.

Files you dropped in or picked resume from the banner, though Chrome may ask you to allow access to them again. Files added another way are chosen again. Drive keeps an unfinished upload for about a week.

Four files at a time, or up to six

Batches finish sooner with several files on the go. Choose 1 to 6 at a time. Big files go up in pieces that grow from 8 MB to 64 MB on a fast connection, and folders keep their structure in Drive.

Drive takes one file's pieces in order, so a single file isn't split. The gain is for batches.

Already in Drive? Skip it, replace it, or keep both

  • Skip it: a file with the same name and size is left alone.
  • Replace it: uploads a new version of the file, so its link and sharing stay the same and Drive keeps the old one in its version history. Files with the same size and date are left alone.
  • Keep both: always uploads a separate copy.

Upload into any folder in My Drive, Shared with me, Starred or a shared drive, chosen in Google's own folder picker.

Next to Drive's own uploader

For one file you could also use drive.google.com, at about the same speed. The difference is what happens when something goes wrong.

What happens drive.google.com on its own With Uploft
You close the tab, or Chrome crashes The upload is lost. You start again. Reopen the uploader and resume from what Drive already has.
Wi‑Fi drops for a few minutes The upload can fail and wait for you to press Retry. It waits for the network, then carries on by itself.
The computer is about to go to sleep Uploads stop while it sleeps. It keeps the computer awake until the uploads finish.

Closing a laptop's lid still puts it to sleep. When it wakes, the uploads continue from where they were.

It also looks after uploads you start in Drive

Prefer to drag files into drive.google.com? Keep doing that. Uploft watches Drive's own upload panel in the tab.

  • Keeps the computer awake while uploads run, then lets it sleep again when they're done.
  • Keeps the Drive tab loaded so Chrome doesn't unload it to save memory mid-upload.
  • Presses Retry for you when an upload fails: after 5 seconds, 20 seconds and a minute, or as soon as the network is back.
  • Tells you when it's done with a progress panel in the tab and a notification when uploads finish or fail.

Up and running in a minute

  1. Add it to Chrome From the Chrome Web Store. It works in Chrome 116 and later.
  2. Sign in with Google Click the toolbar icon, then Upload files to Drive. Google asks you to allow access to the files it uploads, nothing more.
  3. Drop files or folders in They go into an Uploft folder in My Drive, or any folder you pick.

It only sees the files it uploads

Sign-in asks Google for one permission, drive.file. The extension can see the files and folders it creates and the folders you pick, not the rest of your Drive. Files go straight from your browser to Google, with no server of ours in between and no analytics. Read the privacy policy.
